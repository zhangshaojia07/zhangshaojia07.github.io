After months away from OI, I caught the train to Beijing on 11th April to compete in THUPC 2026 the following day.

Poplar catkins were drifting everywhere across Beijing, while I found myself coding like a complete amateur.

It was rather bizarre when a photographer asked us to pose for photos during the contest. It didn't take up much time, but it was a bit distracting.

{{< figure src="awkward-photo.jpg" alt="awkward-photo" position="center" caption="From left to right: yours truly, followed by my brilliant teammates wsyear and Little09." captionPosition="center" >}}

We finished in 11th place after narrowly missing the correct solution to Problem I. It seemed every team was having a go at Problem I, as the operation limits were far too tight. That said, the intended solution was quite brilliant. I was also convinced by the author that Problem B was an excellent, hard-core challenge, even though no team managed to crack it. On a brighter note, Problem L holds a special place for me. It was the only challenge I tackled entirely on my own, from the initial spark of logic to the final 'Accepted' status. I finally solved a geometry problem during a competition.

{{< figure src="standings.png" alt="standings" position="center" caption="Standings" captionPosition="center" >}}

[Standings webpage (QOJ)](https://qoj.ac/contest/3589/standings)

Coming 11th meant a Third Prize, which carries a prize fund of 256 RMB per team (before tax), if I'm not wrong. Regardless of my passion for the game, this trip was hardly a bargain; I spent 1,300 RMB on train tickets and another 1,100 RMB on the hotel. Nevertheless, I'm satisfied with the result.

My teammates and friends didn't have as much free time as I did. Some are now studying at PKU and are in the middle of maths analysis mid-terms. Others are either in the final sprint for NOI or fully immersed in Gaokao revision. I was left to my own devices on the morning of 13th April, wandering around the PKU campus. I couldn't help thinking about how those old friends I used to compete with are now going their separate ways. It might be the last time we all gather like this, or perhaps not. This competition felt like a brief look back at my ten-year competitive career. It feels like the right time to say goodbye to it.
